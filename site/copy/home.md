# Home page copy, draft 1 (2026-11-05)

Story follows the Meet Sthara film. Lead: one living student record. Plain words, British spelling, no em dashes.

## 1. Hero
Eyebrow: THE SCHOOL OS FOR INDIAN SCHOOLS
H1: One living record for every student.
Body: Classwork, homework, attendance, fees, wellbeing and every message home, kept in one place that the student, the teacher, the office and the parent all read from.

Buttons: Book a School Pilot | Watch the 90-second film

## 2. The problem
H2: An exam is a moment. Learning is a pattern.
Body: In most schools that pattern is scattered. Marks sit in a register, homework in notebooks, attendance in one app and updates in a dozen WhatsApp groups. Nobody sees the whole picture, so a gap stays invisible until a report card shows it. By then it has grown.

## 3. One student, four people
H2: One student. Four people who need to know.
Student: Help me learn.
Teacher: Help me teach.
Office: Help me run the school.
Parent: Help me know.
Orbit captions:
- A student's question becomes part of their record.
- A teacher's review becomes the student's next step.
- An entry made once in the office reaches everyone who needs it.
- A school update becomes a useful conversation at home.

## 4. Students
Eyebrow: FOR STUDENTS
H2: A tutor that asks before it answers.
Body: When a student is stuck, the Sthara tutor starts with what they have already tried and guides the next step instead of handing over the answer. It works from the school's own syllabus, and every session adds to the student's record.
Second beat: A private daily check-in lets students say how they are feeling. It only switches on with guardian consent, and it reaches a counsellor who can act on it.
Points: Hints, not answers | The school's own syllabus | Every session counts toward mastery

## 5. Teachers
Eyebrow: FOR TEACHERS
H2: Snap the homework. Confirm the marks.
Body: Photograph a stack of handwritten notebooks. Sthara reads each page and suggests marks question by question. You confirm or change them, and nothing counts until you do.
Second beat: The Teacher Copilot drafts worksheets, quizzes, lesson plans and full board-pattern papers from the CBSE syllabus and your class's actual results. The curriculum map shows what has been taught and what has actually been assessed.
Points: Marking suggestions you confirm | Board-pattern papers in minutes | Taught versus assessed, chapter by chapter

## 6. Live Lab (headline moment)
Eyebrow: LIVE LAB
H2: Run the experiment on the projector. Let the class predict.
Body: 58 interactive simulations for Class 9-10 Science and Class 11-12 Physics. Put one on the smartboard, ask the class what will happen, count the hands, then run it. Every result comes from a tested physics or chemistry engine, not from an AI's guess.
Points:
- All 27 CBSE Class 9-10 practicals, with readings, error analysis, viva questions and the practical record. Class 11-12 Physics practicals too.
- No student devices needed. The class answers by show of hands.
- Set a simulation as homework, and it is marked automatically.
- Wrong predictions show the teacher which misconception to tackle next.
- Every school gets the full library. Teachers can also create new demos with the Copilot: a set number each month on Sthamba, more on Shikhara.
Link: See Live Lab

## 7. True Mastery Level
Eyebrow: TRUE MASTERY LEVEL
H2: Every topic, scored 0 to 100.
Body: Teacher-confirmed homework, quizzes and tutor sessions feed one live score for each topic. Every score shows what it is built from and how sure it is. Teachers see who is slipping and on what. Students see what to practise. Parents see progress without waiting for the report card.
Interactive: keep the current topic dial and evidence chips.
Link: How the score works

## 8. Just ask
Eyebrow: ASK THE SCHOOL OS
H2: Just ask.
Body: Parents and staff can ask a question in plain words and get an answer from the school's own records: marks, attendance, homework, fees and the calendar. Ask in English, Hindi or another Indian language and the answer comes back in the same language.
Example question: How is Kabir doing in Maths this week?
Example answer: Maths: 83%, Proficient. Built from teacher-confirmed homework and quizzes.
Second beat: Parents get a mastery report they can actually read, and school updates arrive on WhatsApp. No new app to learn and no extra work for teachers.

## 9. The whole school
Eyebrow: THE SCHOOL OS
H2: The whole school runs on the same record.
Body: Sthara is also the school's ERP. Everything the office does writes to the same record, so it is entered once and every report that needs it draws from it.
Grid:
- Admissions: online applications, stages, seats and waitlists, RTE
- Fees: collection, reminders, receipts
- Attendance: daily and monthly registers, ready to print
- Exams and report cards: from marks entry to published cards
- Timetables and cover: schedules and substitutions
- Staff: records, leave and payroll with PF, ESI and TDS
- Student records: documents, transfer certificates and Kirti achievements
- CBSE wellness report: drafts itself from data you already have
Line: Admin-side AI grows with your plan.
Link: See the School OS

## 9b. One system, not a bundle of modules (new: the depth of the software)
Eyebrow: UNDER THE HOOD
H2: One system. Not a bundle of apps.
Body: Most school software is separate modules stitched together: a tutoring app, an ERP, a parent app, each with its own copy of the student. Sthara is one system on one database. Nothing is exported, synced or re-typed between parts, because there are no separate parts.
Interactive "follow one mark" chain (animated, click to step through):
1. A teacher confirms one homework mark.
2. The student's True Mastery Level for that topic updates.
3. If that topic is now the student's weakest, the tutor suggests it first.
4. The class heat map shows whether the whole class is stuck on that topic.
5. The parent's mastery report changes, in plain words.
6. The principal's academic health grid moves with it.
Line: One mark, entered once, and six people see the right thing.
Close: Built to the depth of an enterprise system, so the school doesn't have to stitch one together.
